Research

How should a robot act when its partner, or the world it leans on, is only partly known?

A robot sharing a load with a person has to infer their intent from forces and motion. A legged robot bracing against a wall has to decide which contact is worth using. A planner threading an object through tight gaps has to respect geometry it can't simply guess. My projects fall into these three threads.

Human–robot co-transportation

Carrying something together is easy for two people and hard for a person and a robot. The robot sees its partner only through the forces and motion they put into the shared object. I model that uncertainty directly, with planners that anticipate disturbances and controllers that decide when the robot should lead and when it should follow.

The demo shows the idea behind the IROS 2026 paper: the robot leads until the person is persistent enough, then it follows.

IROS 2026First author

Mutual Adaptation in Human-Robot Co-Transportation with Human Preference Uncertainty

Al Jaber Mahmud, Weizi Li, Xuan Wang

Validated with a 20-participant user study and simulations

The robot models its partner's choices as a probability distribution over preferences and tracks a time-varying "stubbornness" measure, so it leads when its plan is sound and yields when the person insists on another path. A pose optimizer handles the unpredictable moments when the human takes the lead.

DARC: Disturbance-Aware Redundant Control for Human–Robot Co-Transportation

Al Jaber Mahmud, Amir Hossain Raj, Duc M. Nguyen, Xuesu Xiao, Xuan Wang

Beat baselines lacking disturbance modeling, pose optimization, or both

The journal extension adds learning to disturbance-aware MPC. A conditional variational autoencoder proposes many arm configurations that reach the same pose, and the one with the lowest predicted control cost is chosen. Tested on a simulated Fetch across trajectories and disturbance levels.

Human-Robot Co-Transportation using Disturbance-Aware MPC with Pose Optimization

Al Jaber Mahmud, Amir Hossain Raj, Duc M. Nguyen, Weizi Li, Xuesu Xiao, Xuan Wang

Demonstrated on a real Fetch mobile manipulator

Unpredictable human behavior and actuator noise act as disturbances on the shared object. At each planning step, the controller estimates each candidate arm configuration's cost under those disturbances by solving a disturbance-aware Riccati equation, then picks the cheapest one and drives the base and arm together.

Human Uncertainty-Aware MPC for Enhanced Human-Robot Collaborative Manipulation

Al Jaber Mahmud, Duc M. Nguyen, Filipe Veiga, Xuesu Xiao, Xuan Wang

An optimal control law with human uncertainty built in

The person's deviations from a nominal trajectory are modeled as a normal distribution with nonzero mean and variance. Folding that model into MPC gives an uncertainty-aware Riccati equation (ua-DARE) whose solution is the optimal control law for an 8-DOF Fetch arm.

Loco-manipulation with support contacts

A legged manipulator can push harder and reach farther if it's allowed to lean on its surroundings. The question is which surface to use and how, since every choice trades force capacity against reach and freedom to move.

Pull harder with and without a brace to see why a support contact matters.

Contact as a Decision Variable: Capability-Tradeoff Contact Selection for Legged Loco-Manipulation

Al Jaber Mahmud, Shuai Li, Xuan Wang

Finds feasible support in 91.8% of task conditions vs 68.4% using the ground alone, about 3× faster than exhaustive search

The robot picks a support surface and a whole-body pose together, scoring each option on leftover wrench, end-effector reach, and base mobility. CTCS avoids solving the full optimization for every candidate: it groups similar ones, predicts their capabilities from a few exact solves, and verifies a shortlist. Tested on a Unitree Go2 with an AgileX NERO arm across 392 task conditions and nine surfaces.

Language models for geometric planning

Language models propose plans quickly, but they don't natively respect geometry. I fine-tune them against a geometric verifier so that what they propose is physically feasible.

Play each plan to see the failure modes a geometric verifier catches.

Under review, ICRA 2027First author

Geometry-Aligned LLM Fine-Tuning for Sequential Narrow-Opening Planning

Al Jaber Mahmud, Xuan Wang

92.6% success in familiar environments and 77.3% in unseen ones, across 1,500 simulated layouts

Moving an object through several tight openings in a row is hard because the pose used for one gap limits the options at the next. The LLM is first fine-tuned with LoRA on demonstrations plus feedback about how its plans fail. GRPO then refines it with rewards from a geometric verifier.
