Research

How should a robot act when its partner, or the world it leans on, is only partly known?

My work sits where physical interaction meets uncertainty. When a robot shares a load with a person, it has to infer their intent from forces and motion. When a legged robot braces against a wall, it has to decide which contacts are worth trusting. And when a planner proposes a path, it has to respect geometry it can't simply guess. These three threads organize the projects below.

Human–robot co-transportation

Carrying an object together is easy for two people and hard for a person and a robot. The robot sees the human only through the forces and motion they put into the shared object. I model that uncertainty directly, with planners that anticipate disturbances and controllers that decide when the robot should lead and when it should follow.

IROS 2026First author

Mutual Adaptation in Human-Robot Co-Transportation with Human Preference Uncertainty

Al Jaber Mahmud, Weizi Li, Xuan Wang

The robot represents its partner's preference as a probability distribution and tracks a time-varying "stubbornness" estimate, so it leads when confident and yields when the person pushes back. A probability-weighted pose optimizer keeps low-level control stable when the human takes over. Validated in simulation and a 20-participant user study.

Electronics 2025First author

DARC: Disturbance-Aware Redundant Control for Human–Robot Co-Transportation

Al Jaber Mahmud, Amir Hossain Raj, Duc M. Nguyen, Xuesu Xiao, Xuan Wang

Extends disturbance-aware MPC with learned components: a CVAE samples redundant arm configurations and picks the one with the lowest predicted control cost, while an LSTM predicts the human's motion. On a Fetch mobile manipulator, DARC achieved lower cumulative cost than baselines missing disturbance modeling, pose optimization, or both, across trajectories and disturbance levels.

  • Model predictive control
  • CVAE
  • LSTM motion prediction
  • Redundancy resolution

Human-Robot Co-Transportation using Disturbance-Aware MPC with Pose Optimization

Al Jaber Mahmud, Amir Hossain Raj, Duc M. Nguyen, Weizi Li, Xuesu Xiao, Xuan Wang

An MPC planner that explicitly models disturbances from the human partner, paired with proactive pose optimization that keeps the mobile manipulator in configurations best able to absorb them. Demonstrated on a Fetch robot.

Loco-manipulation with support contacts

A legged manipulator can push harder and reach farther if it's allowed to brace against its surroundings. I'm building whole-body planners that decide where and how the robot should make support contacts, along with the ROS 2 stack that runs them on real hardware.

OngoingHardware

CTCS: Whole-body optimization for environmental support contacts

Selects support contacts by scoring each candidate on wrench, reach, and mobility capability. Evaluated on a Unitree Go2 carrying an AgileX NERO arm, in simulation and on hardware across 392 tasks, running 3× faster than exhaustive search. Includes ROS 2 packages for arm actuation, robot-state feedback, command validation, and Go2 integration.

Language models for geometric planning

Language models propose plans quickly, but they don't natively respect geometry. I fine-tune them against geometric verifiers so that what they propose is physically feasible.

arXiv 2026First author

Geometry-Aligned LLM Fine-Tuning for Sequential Narrow-Opening Planning

Al Jaber Mahmud, Xuan Wang

Moving a rigid body through a chain of tight openings, where the pose used for one passage constrains the next. The LLM is LoRA fine-tuned on failure-annotated demonstrations, then trained with GRPO against a geometric verifier on waypoint plans densified by a model-based planner. It reached 92.6% success in-distribution and 77.3% out-of-distribution across 1,500 simulated environments.

  • LLM fine-tuning
  • LoRA
  • GRPO
  • Motion planning
